# Removing AI-sounding patterns

Adapted from `writing-anti-ai` (Galaxy-Dawn/claude-scholar, MIT), which itself draws on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide. Rewritten for research papers.

Language models pick the most statistically likely wording. The result is text that fits everywhere and says little. Fix it with specifics, not with synonyms.

## Content patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Inflated significance ("plays a crucial role", "marks a pivotal shift", "stands as a testament") | State what happened and the number |
| Promotional words ("novel", "powerful", "remarkable", "state-of-the-art") | Delete unless a result supports it, then give the result |
| Vague attribution ("studies show", "experts argue", "recent work suggests") | Name the paper and cite it, or delete |
| Tacked-on -ing phrases ("...highlighting the importance of...", "...ensuring robustness") | Cut, or turn into a plain sentence with a fact |
| Formula "Despite X, challenges remain" | Name the specific limitation and its cause |
| Summary sentences that repeat the paragraph | Delete |

## Language patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Words such as "delve", "tapestry", "landscape", "intricate", "pivotal", "underscore", "foster", "leverage", "showcase" | Use a plain word, or cut |
| "Additionally", "Moreover", "Furthermore" opening many sentences | Drop them, or join the ideas |
| "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" | "is", "has" |
| "It is not just X, it is Y" | "X does Y" |
| Lists of three everywhere | Use the real number of items |
| Changing the name of the same thing every sentence | Pick one term and keep it |
| Em-dashes and double dashes | Commas, periods or parentheses |
| Bold in running text, bold-header bullet lists in prose sections | Plain sentences |

## Filler to cut

- "it is important to note that" (delete)
- "due to the fact that" (because)
- "in order to" (to)
- "a wide range of" (name them, or "many")
- "in today's rapidly evolving field" (delete)
- "plays a key role in" (name the role)
- "has the potential to" (say what was shown, or hedge once)
- "it should be noted" (delete)

## Hedging

Hedge once, at the claim, in proportion to the evidence. "It could potentially be argued that the method may possibly help" becomes "The method may help". Stacked hedges read as evasion.

## Voice

Removing patterns is half the job. Flat text with no voice is also obvious. Use "we" in the usual way for the field. Say what surprised you when it did. Prefer a concrete sentence over a smooth one. Vary sentence length. Do not end every paragraph on a neat summary line.

## Score

Rate 1 to 10 on each, then fix the lowest:
- Directness: states things or announces them
- Rhythm: varied or metronomic
- Trust: respects the reader or over-explains
- Authenticity: sounds like a person who ran this work
- Density: anything left to cut

Below 35 of 50, revise. 45 and above is good.

## Limits

Do not change numbers, citations, terminology fixed by the field, or the author's meaning. Do not add opinions the evidence does not support just to sound human. Style is never a reason to remove a hedge the data requires.
